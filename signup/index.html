What it measures

Four signals that tell you what is happening, without watching anyone.

dB

Noise level

Loudness only. You set the limit for quiet hours; Stillroom waits for it to stay high before it alerts you, so one slammed door is not a party.

40 dB quiet flat · 60 dB lively talk · 75+ dB party
ppm

CO₂

Every person in a room breathes out CO₂. A sharp, steady climb means more people than the booking, even when nobody is loud.

~420 ppm outdoor air · 1,000+ ppm crowded room
PM2.5

Smoke

Cigarette smoke fills the air with fine particles. A sudden spike means someone is smoking inside, even if they open a window afterwards.

Under 15 µg/m³ clean air · 100+ µg/m³ smoking
°C · %

Temperature and humidity

Catches heating left on full while guests are out, and damp air that turns into mould, like long showers with the fan off.

Moved?

Tamper alert

A motion sensor inside knows when the sensor is lifted, turned or carried off, and alerts you at once. A guest cannot quietly put it in a cupboard before the party. It senses the sensor itself, not the people in the room.

How it works

Set it up once. It looks after the guests from there.

  1. Set it up

    Put the sensor in the living room or another shared space and hang the screen where guests will see it. Both join the rental's Wi-Fi. Add what guests need: Wi-Fi details and checkout time.

  2. Set your house rules

    Quiet hours, a noise limit, no smoking and the number of guests in the booking.

  3. Guests are asked first

    When quiet hours are broken, the screen asks guests to turn it down. You get an alert with the readings if it carries on for 10 minutes, or straight away if someone smokes or moves the device.

Private by design

Your guests are measured as numbers, never recorded.

Questions

Before you sign up

When does Stillroom launch?

We are building the first units now. Everyone on the waitlist hears first, before we open orders to anyone else.

What will it cost?

We will share the price with the waitlist before launch. Signing up does not commit you to anything.

Will guests mind a screen in the room?

It is built to be useful to them first: the Wi-Fi, checkout time and when to open a window, the things guests usually message you about. It only asks them to quiet down when quiet hours are broken, and it tells them before it tells you.

Do I have to tell my guests?

Yes. Airbnb requires hosts to disclose noise monitors in the listing. You do not have to say exactly where it is.

How does it know someone is smoking?

It measures fine particles (PM2.5) in the air. Cigarette smoke makes them jump within minutes, far above what cooking or dust does over the same time, so it needs no camera.

What if a guest moves or hides it?

You get an alert the moment the sensor is lifted or moved. Its motion sensor only feels the sensor being handled; it does not detect people walking around.

Why CO₂ and not a people counter?

People counters need a camera or have to track guests' phones. CO₂ rises with the number of people breathing in the room, so it shows a crowd without identifying anyone.
